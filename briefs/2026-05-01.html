Records on Broadening Leadership — The Russell Outpaces the Nasdaq, Industrials Carry the Tape
The S&P 500 advanced 0.99% to roughly 7,187, a fresh all-time closing high per Yahoo Finance and CNBC characterization of the session, with the Dow up 1.63% and the Russell 2000 leading at +2.16%. The notable feature is the breadth: ten of eleven sectors finished green, with industrials at +2.74%, utilities at +2.56%, healthcare at +2.21%, real estate at +1.74%, and staples at +1.68% all outperforming technology, which posted only a 0.25% advance. NVDA fell 4.63% to $199.57 even as the index traded to records — an unusual configuration that confirms Wednesday’s rotation has now resolved into a non-tech rally rather than a broad de-risking. UVXY compressed 4.02%, and the small-cap leadership pattern is consistent with positioning that has rotated toward cyclicals, financials-adjacent (XLF +0.40%), and rate-sensitive defensives. Carson’s SPY support at $645.80 remains active and unchallenged with the index well above the level.
CAT Posts a Material Beat and Raises Guidance — The Industrial-AI-Datacenter Linkage Is Now Quantified
Caterpillar reported Q1 adjusted EPS of $5.54 against $4.66 consensus per the data drop, with Yahoo Finance and Benzinga reporting revenue growth of 22% to $17.42B versus $16.61B expected. Construction Industries sales rose 30% to $7.2B with segment margin expanding 160 basis points, while Power and Energy revenue advanced 22%. Management raised full-year sales guidance to low double-digit growth and trimmed the projected tariff hit to $2.2B–$2.4B from $2.6B prior, citing IIJA-supported infrastructure spend and data-center construction as the principal demand drivers per the company’s prepared remarks. The print took CAT up 9.88% to $890.11 on the session and provides concrete read-across to STLD (+1.91%), FAST (+2.79%), GPC (+3.82%), PCAR (+0.56%), and the broader IOWN industrial-physical-AI thesis. The 9.88% rally also confirms the rotation framework: capital is moving from multiple-driven AI semiconductors into the cash-flow-real-asset providers that monetize the physical buildout.
QCOM Q2 Surges 15% on AI-Edge Beat — SYK Misses on Cyber Disruption, the Dispersion Theme Holds
QCOM posted Q2 EPS of $2.65 against $2.61 consensus per the data drop, a clean beat that took the stock up 15.12% to $179.58 with an intraday range of $163.56–$186.89 and trading volume confirming institutional repositioning per Yahoo Finance and Finnhub coverage. The reported OpenAI-MediaTek custom-chip optionality that the SeekingAlpha Q2 preview flagged as the central narrative drove the print’s reception, although the structural Apple modem-insourcing risk and 2027 royalty cliff remain in place. SYK was the contrast point in the dividend sleeve: Q1 EPS of $2.60 versus $3.01 expected, a material miss that the SeekingAlpha and Yahoo Finance call transcripts attribute to cyber-related operational disruption during the quarter. SYK closed unchanged at $315.13 as the print had been telegraphed. CAT, GD (+1.64% after the company raised its 2026 EPS guidance on Marine segment strength per Yahoo Finance), CTRA, and VLO (+0.51% on the refining-margin beat) round out the IOWN reporting cohort — four of five names beat consensus with only SYK falling short.

Geopolitics

Iran Truce Holds, Oil Pulls Back — The War-Powers Deadline Is Now the Cleanest Catalyst
USO declined 2.35% to $147.09 and BNO eased 0.48% as a US official confirmed to Reuters that the Iran ceasefire has terminated hostilities for the purposes of the War Powers Act deadline, removing one of the structural risk premia that had supported crude through April. CNBC reporting Friday morning indicates Brent settled at $114.01 after briefly touching a four-year high earlier in the week, while CBS News notes that Iran has refused to reopen the Strait of Hormuz unless the US lifts its blockade of Iranian ports. The truce holds, but the Strait remains effectively impeded and both sides have continued seizing vessels accused of violating restrictions per CBS coverage. The IOWN energy sleeve diverged: CVX advanced 0.57% to $193.31 ahead of this morning’s print, OKE rallied 3.52% on a Scotiabank downgrade reversal, VLO held +0.51% on Q1 refining-margin strength, while CNX softened 1.04%. Natural gas (UNG +4.43%) was the clear commodity outperformer as the war-related disruption to LNG arbitrage routes remains in place.
CTRA Exits the S&P 500 May 7 — Devon Merger Closes the Loop on a $58B Shale Combination
S&P Dow Jones Indices announced after Wednesday’s close that Veeva Systems will replace Coterra Energy in the S&P 500 effective prior to the open on May 7, with Devon Energy’s acquisition of Coterra in an all-stock $58B transaction expected to close in Q2 2026 per Devon’s investor materials and CNBC coverage. The combination creates a Permian-focused operator with pro forma production exceeding 1.6 million barrels of oil equivalent per day and targeted annual pre-tax merger synergies of approximately $1.0B once realized per the merger announcement. CTRA closed up 0.64% to $35.91 on the session, with the index-removal mechanic likely to drive forced selling from passive S&P trackers into next Wednesday’s close. The implication for the IOWN dividend sleeve: CTRA holders will receive Devon shares at the merger close, materially reshaping the energy sub-sleeve’s composition. The transaction also confirms the consolidation thesis: capital is rewarding scale operators in the Delaware Basin, and the Coterra exit is a clean read-through to the structural pricing pressure that justifies the broader IOWN energy weighting.
Hassett Outlines Fertilizer Cost-Reduction Plan — NTR Sits at the Center of a Multi-Agency Push
National Economic Council Director Kevin Hassett joined Interior Secretary Burgum, Commerce Secretary Lutnick, EPA Administrator Zeldin, and USDA Secretary Rollins to outline a coordinated administration push to reduce fertilizer costs ahead of the spring planting season per Brownfield Ag News, DTN, and Hoosier Ag Today coverage. The plan targets a 30% expansion in domestic nitrogen production and over 200% expansion in domestic phosphate production over the next 12–24 months, supported by streamlined permitting and trade-policy adjustments per the announcement. Nutrien officials announced support for lifting the countervailing duties currently applied to certain phosphate imports, distinguishing the company’s posture from competitor Mosaic, which continues to back the duties. NTR closed up 2.52% to $76.00 on the session as Benzinga reporting confirmed the policy direction. The IOWN materials and agricultural-input exposure runs primarily through NTR and tangentially through FCX (+1.49%) on the broader commodity pull — the policy framework is constructive for domestic producers willing to take share from the import channel.
