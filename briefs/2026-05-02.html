Section 02

Geopolitics

Iran Sends Updated Peace Proposal Through Pakistan — Trump Publicly Unsatisfied
Iran confirmed Friday that it has delivered an updated peace proposal to Pakistani mediators per CNN, CNBC, and OANN reporting, with President Trump telling reporters “I’m not satisfied with it, so we’ll see what happens” and indicating that Tehran’s requests include items he cannot accept. The structural sticking points remain Iran’s nuclear and ballistic programs, the status of the Strait of Hormuz, and reconstruction-and-sanctions framing per House of Commons Library characterization of the Islamabad track. A US official separately confirmed to Reuters Friday that the absence of direct US-Iran fire since the April 7 ceasefire means the 60-day War Powers clock no longer applies, removing one of the structural risk premia that supported crude through April. USO closed Friday at $147.09 (↓2.35%) and BNO at $58.53 (↓0.48%); CNBC reported WTI cash at $101.94 and Brent at $108.17 to close the week. The IOWN energy sleeve’s diversified posture — integrated (CVX, VLO), midstream (OKE), upstream (CNX, CTRA), and ag-input (NTR) — continues to provide layered exposure to a backdrop in which the truce holds but the Hormuz transit picture remains effectively impeded.
CVX Q1 Adjusted Beat, Downstream Timing Charge as Disclosed — Refining Throughput at Record
Chevron reported Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $1.41 against $1.17 consensus per the Business Wire release and StockStory coverage, with US refinery crude unit throughput exceeding 1 million barrels per day for the fifth consecutive quarter and reaching a record in March 2026. The downstream segment swung to a reported $817 million loss versus a $325 million prior-year profit, a swing the company attributed primarily to approximately $3 billion of unfavorable timing effects from rapid commodity-price increases per the prepared remarks — the same noisy-quarter framework SeekingAlpha and Benzinga flagged in the previews. The company returned $6 billion to shareholders during the quarter and reaffirmed the structural cost-savings program targeting $3–$4 billion by year-end 2026. The print closed the loop on the IOWN energy reporting cohort: CAT (+9.88% Friday on the industrial-AI build read), VLO (+0.51% on refining-margin strength), CTRA (+0.64% into the Devon merger), CNX (+1.21% beat), and now CVX with timing-cleansed underlying earnings power. The integrated-energy thesis survives the timing noise.
Consumer Staples — CL and CHD Both Beat, Dispersion Narrows Within the Defensive Sleeve
Colgate-Palmolive posted Q1 adjusted EPS of $0.97 against $0.95 consensus on revenue of $5.32B versus $5.20B expected per Investing.com and Yahoo Finance reporting, with organic sales up 2.9% and gross margin compressing 130 basis points to 60.6% on raw-material and packaging cost pressure. Church & Dwight reported Q1 adjusted EPS of $0.95 against $0.92 consensus per Motley Fool transcript coverage, with organic sales up 5.0% (well ahead of the 3.0% guidance) on 5.3% volume growth, and the company maintained full-year adjusted EPS growth guidance of 5%–8%. CL closed Friday at $85.36 (+1.03%) and CHD at $97.06 (+0.89%) per the data drop. Both prints validate the dividend sleeve’s pricing-power and category-share thesis — volume growth at CHD is the cleaner read into a tariff-and-input-cost environment, and Colgate’s margin slip is a flag worth monitoring against the same ISM prices-paid backdrop.
