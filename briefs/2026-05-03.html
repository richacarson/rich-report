Berkshire’s $397.4B Cash Pile Frames the Investment-Committee Question
Berkshire Hathaway disclosed Saturday at its 2026 annual meeting that the conglomerate’s cash position reached a record $397.4 billion at quarter-end, exceeding the prior $381.6B high set in Q3 2025 per CNBC, Bloomberg, Reuters, and Fortune coverage of new CEO Greg Abel’s first annual meeting. Q1 operating earnings rose 18% to $11.35B from $9.64B per the company release, and net income more than doubled to $10.18B. BNSF profit advanced 13% to $1.38B on stronger grain, petroleum, and oilseed demand, and Berkshire Hathaway Energy edged up 2% on natural-gas pipeline revenue. The cash accumulation under Abel — held primarily at the front of the curve — is itself a market signal: Berkshire continues to find the post-Mag7-print, record-ATH environment difficult to source value-oriented acquisitions in. The Buffett anti-Bitcoin stance was reaffirmed by Abel per Yahoo Finance coverage. For an RIA committee evaluating allocation, the posture suggests selective, valuation-disciplined deployment over chasing strength. The IOWN dividend sleeve’s emphasis on cash-flow industrials and real-asset producers (CAT, GD, ADP, NEE, ATO, GPC, FAST) maps onto the same framework.
When the largest single discretionary allocator in US equities holds $397B in cash at fresh all-time highs, the question is not whether to deploy but how to discriminate. Think Like an Owner.

Hormuz Blockade Holds Through the Weekend — The War-Powers Termination Did Not Reopen the Strait
Bloomberg reporting Saturday confirmed the Iran-related blockade in Hormuz continues to constrain the world’s most important waterway, with the Strait remaining roughly 90% closed and approximately 20,000 seafarers stranded across affected vessels per the same coverage and parallel reporting. Friday’s data drop showed USO declining 2.35% to $147.09 and BNO easing 0.48% as oil markets digested a US official’s confirmation to Reuters that the War Powers Act deadline had been treated as terminated despite ongoing physical impedance — the legal framework moved while the disruption persisted. CNBC coverage indicates Brent settled near $111 after touching higher levels earlier in the week, logging a second consecutive weekly gain. Trump’s notification to Congress March 2 set the 60-day clock that expired May 1, and the administration declared hostilities terminated rather than seeking authorization or withdrawing per CNBC and Reuters. Approximately 45 commercial vessels have been turned back by the US naval blockade of Iranian ports since April 13 per the same coverage. The integrated-energy thesis remains intact: refining margins are materially elevated, and IOWN names CVX (Q1 reported Friday), VLO (Q1 beat), CNX, and OKE retain forward exposure to a duration-extending supply premium that the formal terminology did not relieve.

2. The Warsh confirmation moves to a full Senate vote the week of May 11, with Powell’s term as chair expiring May 15. The Senate Banking Committee approved Warsh on a 13–11 strict party-line vote Wednesday per CNBC, Bloomberg, and Reuters — the first fully partisan committee vote on a Fed chair nominee. With Republicans holding 53 seats, Warsh requires only a simple majority and Senator Fetterman has indicated bipartisan support per Semafor. The 8–4 FOMC dissent — the largest in 34 years per CNBC — underscores the institutional resistance Warsh will face in pivoting the rate path; while the chair sets the agenda, the chair holds only one of twelve votes. Warsh has publicly committed not to operate as the administration’s “sock puppet” per CNN coverage of his confirmation hearing. The IOWN dividend sleeve’s rate-sensitive defensives — ATO, BKH, NEE, DGX — carry the principal sensitivity to the framing transition. Avoid Erosion.
3. CTRA exits the S&P 500 effective prior to Thursday’s May 7 open as the Devon merger closes the loop on a $58B all-stock combination. S&P Dow Jones Indices announced Veeva Systems will replace Coterra Energy in the index per Yahoo and Benzinga reporting, with Devon’s acquisition of Coterra expected to close in Q2 2026 per Devon’s investor materials. The combination would create a Permian-focused operator with pro forma production exceeding 1.6 million barrels of oil equivalent per day and targeted annual pre-tax merger synergies of approximately $1.0B once realized per the merger announcement. CTRA holders would receive Devon shares at the close, materially reshaping the IOWN energy sub-sleeve’s composition. Passive S&P tracker rebalancing flows tend to be the principal mechanical driver of price action ahead of an index removal of this size, and the May 6 close into the May 7 open is the operative window.
