Section 02

Geopolitics

Hormuz Exchange Continues Into the Weekend — Iran’s Framework Response Pending, Trump–Xi Five Days Out
The Hormuz track entered the weekend with the framework response still outstanding from Tehran. Per CNN, Al Jazeera, and Times of Israel coverage, Secretary Rubio publicly stated the US expects Iran’s reply to the one-page, fourteen-point memorandum within the Friday window, and Iranian Fars news reported “sporadic clashes” between Iranian and US naval forces in the Strait through Friday. The proposed framework would declare an end to the war launched on February 28, open a thirty-day window for detailed negotiations, and lift the Iranian-imposed restrictions on Hormuz transit that have been in place since early March per Axios reporting. Friday’s incidents continued to test it: per CNBC and PBS reporting, Iran fired ballistic missiles and drones at the UAE (three wounded per the UAE Defense Ministry), and US forces disabled two Iranian tankers attempting to evade the Gulf-of-Oman blockade. The Trump–Xi Beijing summit on May 14–15 now sits five days out and, per CNBC and SCMP coverage, will frame Iran rather than tariffs or rare earths as the principal agenda item. Brent (BNO) closed Friday at $53.11 (–1.04% on the day) for a weekly decline of roughly 6% per CNBC and TradingEconomics coverage of weekly losses; the energy complex appears to be discounting a constructive framework outcome rather than the ongoing exchange.
Sanctions Tighten as the Diplomatic Track Strains — The Framework Window is Narrowing Rather Than Closing
Per Reuters reporting Friday, the US imposed fresh sanctions on entities accused of aiding Iran’s weapons sector and revised its UN resolution on Iran with China and Russia still expected to veto, while Secretary Rubio publicly questioned allies’ support on the Iran file following his Italy talks. Reuters separately reported a suspected oil spill near Iran’s Kharg Island export hub visible on satellite imagery, and that Russia’s Lavrov urged the UAE to support the US–Iran talks. Citi commented that oil could rise further if the talks remain thorny per Reuters, and Russia’s diplomatic outreach indicates the broader great-power architecture is now actively engaged in the framework outcome. The IOWN defense exposure (LMT $506.51 –1.15% Fri, GD $346.53 –0.35% Fri) finished marginally softer in a week where the framework-breakdown hedge has been priced down on the diplomatic-progress assumption; in a scenario where the framework is rejected and the Hormuz exchange escalates, the sleeve carries the most direct re-pricing optionality.
