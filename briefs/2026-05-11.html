Friday Printed a Record on the NFP Beat — Sunday Reset the Setup
The S&P 500 closed Friday at 7,398.93 (+0.84%) and the Nasdaq Composite at 26,247.08 (+1.71%), both fresh record closes, with the Dow essentially flat at 49,609.16 (+0.02%) and the Russell 2000 +0.76% to 2,861.19 per CNBC and TheStreet coverage. On the week, the S&P added 2.3% and registered a sixth-straight winning week — the longest such streak since 2024 per CNBC. The catalyst was the April Employment Situation: nonfarm payrolls rose 115K against a +62K Wall Street consensus per the BLS release, with the unemployment rate unchanged at 4.3% and average hourly earnings cooling to +0.2% MoM and +3.6% YoY versus +3.5% YoY prior. The wage moderation is the cleaner read for the rate path; the headline beat preserves the soft-landing framework into Tuesday’s April CPI release. The tape on Friday also absorbed a fresh US-Iran exchange in the Hormuz corridor without ceding the AI-led bid, consistent with the past two weeks’ pattern of geopolitical headlines being repriced inside an intact growth narrative.
Sunday Reversal — Trump Rejects Iran’s Counter-Proposal, Crude Reclaims the Bid
Iran submitted a counter-proposal Sunday morning that, per Al Jazeera, Reuters, and Business Standard reporting, called for Iranian sovereignty over the Strait of Hormuz, compensation for war damages, release of frozen Iranian assets, sanctions relief, and an end to hostilities on all fronts including Lebanon. Trump responded Sunday evening via Truth Social rejecting Iran’s position outright per CNN and OilPrice reporting. The energy complex repriced immediately: Brent climbed approximately 3.3% to $104.60 and WTI approximately 3.3% to $98.62 per OilPrice and The National coverage, materially reversing the prior week’s 6% decline that had priced an imminent framework. ES futures traded near 7,407 (–0.16%) and NQ futures softened roughly 0.2% Sunday night per CNBC. The committee may reasonably treat the Monday open as a reverse mirror of last Wednesday’s session: the energy sleeve and defense exposure carry forward, the growth sleeve faces a tactical headwind, and the framework window has narrowed without closing.

Section 02

Geopolitics

Iran’s Counter-Proposal — Hormuz Sovereignty, War-Damages Compensation, Lebanon Linkage
Per Al Jazeera, Business Standard, and Reuters coverage, Iran’s Sunday submission to the US plan reframed the framework in three material ways: it asserted Iranian sovereignty over the Strait of Hormuz rather than accepting a multilateral transit arrangement, it demanded compensation for war damages and the release of frozen Iranian assets, and it extended the conflict-termination clause to include Lebanon and other regional fronts. Trump’s Sunday-evening response via Truth Social rejected the position outright per CNN and OilPrice reporting. The reframing aligns with the senior Iranian commentary that closed last week — Mohsen Rezaee on Hormuz and the senior parliamentarian characterization of the US framework as an American wish list — and is consistent with Tehran electing to negotiate on its terms ahead of the Trump–Xi summit rather than concede them. The committee may reasonably treat the framework window as further narrowed without being closed: the Pakistan-mediated channel remains open, but the gap on Hormuz custody, sanctions architecture, and frozen-asset release has now been formally articulated.
Trump–Xi Beijing Summit Three Days Out — Iran Oil and Rare Earths Lead the Architecture
Per CSIS, Brookings, and CFR coverage, the Trump–Xi summit on May 14–15 in Beijing now sits three days from today and arrives against a substantially harder Iran backdrop. CNBC and Bloomberg reporting indicate the principal US focus will be Chinese purchases of Iranian oil — a material funding channel for Tehran — alongside concerns about potential Chinese arms supplies to Iran. The broader summit agenda covers trade-truce extension, the rare-earth and US-goods purchase architecture, AI cooperation parameters, and Taiwan. Per the Voice of Emirates summit readout, the announced deliverables are expected to include a bilateral Board of Trade for non-sensitive sector purchase commitments and limited tariff adjustments. The IOWN defense exposure (LMT, GD) and the energy sleeve (CVX, VLO, OKE) carry the principal sensitivity to summit outcomes: a constructive Iran-oil framework with Beijing would compress the energy premium that Sunday just restored, while a stalled outcome would extend it.
Drone Activity Across the Gulf — UAE Air-Defense Engagement Volumes Are Notable
Per Al Jazeera and CBC coverage, Iran conducted drone strikes against Qatar, the UAE, and Kuwait over the weekend, following Tehran’s threats to target American sites and enemy ships if its tankers were attacked. UAE authorities disclosed that air defenses have engaged 550 ballistic missiles, nearly 30 cruise missiles, and over 2,200 drones since Iran began regional strikes earlier this year per the same coverage. The volumes are a quantitative anchor for what has been an episodic geopolitical news flow: the regional integrated air-and-missile-defense posture is now a sustained operational tempo rather than an event-driven response. Per Reuters reporting last week, Secretary Rubio approved $25.8B in weapons sales to Middle East nations — a forward marker for the defense backlog independent of how the framework ultimately resolves. The committee may reasonably observe that the defense-sleeve thesis is reinforced by both the framework-breakdown scenario and the underlying multi-year air-defense replenishment cycle.

On Our Radar

1. April CPI prints Tuesday at 7:30 AM CT — the single most consequential macro release of the week. Per the BLS release schedule, the April CPI report publishes Tuesday May 12 at 8:30 AM ET. Kiplinger and TradingKey consensus indicate headline CPI at +0.6% MoM and +3.7% YoY, with core CPI at +0.3% MoM and +2.7% YoY. The print sits against a hot March comparison — the March headline at +0.9% MoM was the largest monthly increase since June 2022, with energy contributing 10.9% MoM and gasoline +21.2% per BLS. The April reading will frame whether the energy-driven inflation impulse is persistent or transient and is the cleanest near-term read on the rate path. With April AHE having cooled to +3.6% YoY in Friday’s payrolls release, a softer CPI core would compound the constructive labor read; a hotter print would re-establish the inflation-impulse channel that the March data introduced. Note: with the April publication, several CPI series rebase to December 2024 = 100 per BLS guidance — the committee should expect a brief presentation-format adjustment in headline coverage. 4. April NFP retrospective — the wage-growth slowdown is the more durable signal than the headline beat. The April nonfarm payrolls release on Friday came in at +115K against a +62K Wall Street consensus per BLS — a clean beat but below the +185K March print. Job gains concentrated in health care (+37K), transportation and warehousing (+30K), and retail trade (+22K), while federal-government employment continued to decline. Average hourly earnings rose 6 cents (+0.2% MoM) to $37.41, with year-over-year growth at +3.6% — a cooling versus prior trend. Per Stephens commentary and CNBC coverage, the print preserves the soft-landing framework: hiring at the small and large ends of the business spectrum continues, while the wage moderation reduces the inflation-impulse channel through the labor market.
