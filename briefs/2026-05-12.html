Geopolitics

Day 74 — Trump Rejects “Garbage” Iranian Counterproposal; Hormuz Stays Effectively Closed Into the Beijing Summit
Per CNN and Reuters reporting, Trump described the Iranian counterproposal as “garbage” and characterized the ceasefire as on “massive life support” on Sunday into Monday, with the deadlock concentrated on uranium custody, sanctions architecture, and Strait of Hormuz transit. Reuters separately reported that the Strait remains effectively closed, with almost no commercial shipping passage, and that the battle for Hormuz risks sparking the next round of direct exchange between the two sides. Saudi Aramco CEO Amin Nasser warned per Reuters that the global market may not normalize until 2027 if the disruption persists. The IOWN energy sleeve repriced higher on the day: VLO +2.93% to $248.13, CVX +1.72% to $184.74, OKE +3.09% to $87.79, and the upstream/midstream complex generally firm; defense was mixed (LMT +1.13% to $512.25, GD –0.72% to $344.03) as the cyclical rotation absorbed more flow than the geopolitical bid.
Trump-Xi Beijing Summit Now 48 Hours Out — Powell’s Term Expires Friday
Per CSIS, Washington Post, and Atlantic Council coverage, the Trump-Xi summit on May 14-15 in Beijing will sit against an active Iran exchange rather than a stabilizing ceasefire, with Treasury Secretary Bessent having already framed Iran as a primary topic; CNBC reported the Iran focus may delay progress on tariffs and rare earths. A US CEO delegation that reportedly includes Tesla’s Elon Musk, Apple’s Tim Cook, and Boeing’s Kelly Ortberg travels with the President per CFR coverage. Separately, Jerome Powell’s term as Fed Chair expires Friday May 15 per the Federal Reserve calendar; Kevin Warsh’s Senate Banking Committee nomination advanced 13–11 on April 29 per Al Jazeera and is awaiting a full Senate vote. Powell stated on April 29 that he will remain on the Board after stepping down as chair per CNBC and NPR coverage. The combination of an active Hormuz exchange, the Beijing summit, a CPI print today, and a Fed chair transition Friday compresses a substantial amount of macro-political risk into the next four sessions.
