Hot CPI Breaks the Record Run — Bond Yields Reprice to a One-Year High, Defensive Rotation Replaces Reflation
April CPI printed materially above consensus and reset the policy path for the first time since the rally began. Per the Bureau of Labor Statistics release and Reuters coverage, headline CPI rose 0.6% MoM and 3.8% YoY (vs. 3.7% expected), the highest annual print since May 2023; core CPI rose 0.4% MoM and 2.8% YoY (vs. 2.7% expected), also a multi-month high. Energy was the dominant driver per the BLS breakdown: energy prices climbed 17.9% YoY (the steepest annual increase since September 2022), gasoline 28.4% YoY, and fuel oil 54.3% YoY — the BLS breakdown is consistent with the Iran-war energy impulse flowing through to the headline inflation series. SPY closed at $738.18 (–0.15%) with an intraday low of $731.83 and the Nasdaq (QQQ) shed 0.85% to $707.24, breadth tilting negative as the Russell 2000 (IWM) lagged at –0.97%; the Dow (DIA) held marginally green at +0.16%. The session signature was a clean defensive rotation: Healthcare (XLV) +1.96%, Staples (XLP) +1.28%, Financials (XLF) +0.78%, while Tech (XLK) –1.51% and Discretionary (XLY) –0.90% absorbed the rate shock. Treasury yields repriced sharply per Reuters and CNBC coverage of the session, with the 10-year approaching one-year highs and the 2-year moving back toward 4.0%; implied rate-market pricing per Polymarket coverage suggested roughly a 62% probability of zero Fed cuts in 2026 with better-than-1-in-3 odds priced for a hike by year-end, and TheStreet reported Bank of America moving its first-cut forecast into 2027 from its prior September 2026 view.
QCOM Gives Back —11.46%, CRDO —5.54%, but NVDA Holds Up at +0.61% — the Chip Complex Splits on Rate Sensitivity
The chip complex absorbed the bulk of the rotation but the move was non-uniform. QCOM gave back essentially all of Monday’s record post-earnings advance, closing at $210.31 (–11.46%) with an intraday low of $202.00 after Monday’s $237.53 (+8.42%) record per the data drop and ChartMill coverage. CRDO closed at $198.57 (–5.54%) after Monday’s +11.52% advance. AMD declined 2.29% to $448.29, MRVL –3.71% to $164.50, NXPI –3.84% to $294.23, TSM –1.79% to $397.28, and LRCX –2.30% to $289.24. The notable exception was NVDA, which finished +0.61% at $220.78 with an intraday high of $223.75 — the connectivity and design-tool layer absorbed the rotation while the core compute name held. The sell-side stance remained constructive into NVDA’s May 20 print per coverage of Wells Fargo and Susquehanna research notes raising their price targets. SeekingAlpha’s Tuesday coverage featured a piece on broader semiconductor weakness and a separate piece on CRDO arguing against capitulation — the analyst tone tracks a sell-side reading of the move as a valuation reset rather than a thesis break.
Energy Reflation Persists in the Commodity Tape — USO +4.07%, BNO +3.40% — While the Equity Sector Underperforms on Discount-Rate Pressure
Oil extended its move on the Iran-Hormuz escalation: WTI ETF (USO) closed +4.07% at $144.30 and Brent ETF (BNO) +3.40% at $56.89 per the data drop, with Bloomberg and CNBC reporting Brent and WTI futures settling sharply higher into the session. President Trump’s sharply critical characterization of the ceasefire on Monday per ABC and Fox coverage, paired with Reuters reporting that the President framed halting Iran’s nuclear program as outweighing the near-term economic cost on Americans, suggests a policy posture willing to absorb a sustained energy-inflation impulse. Notably, the equity Energy sector (XLE) only added 0.70% even as crude advanced 3–4% — a sign that the rate-shock weighed on the multiple even as the spot tape supported the revenue line. Within the IOWN energy sleeve, the pattern was selective: CVX +0.66% to $185.95, OKE +0.73% to $88.43, LMT +1.71% to $521.00 (defense bid), and FCX +2.58% to $66.03 (copper/gold complex), while VLO declined 0.41% to $247.12 on profit-taking after Monday’s +2.93% move. UVXY (a leveraged short-term VIX futures ETF) declined 3.35% to $35.74 despite the rate shock — positioning appears to have already absorbed substantial protection bids into the print. Fear & Greed moved to 42 (Fear) from the 49 (Neutral) reading carried in Monday’s brief. Gold (GLD) eased 0.40% to $432.93; Silver (SLV) added 0.71% to $78.55.

Geopolitics

Warsh Confirmation Vote Today — Powell’s Term Ends Friday into the Beijing Summit Tomorrow
The Fed transition has compressed onto today’s session. Per CNBC, US News, and CoinDesk reporting on Tuesday’s Senate floor action, the Senate confirmed Kevin Warsh to the Federal Reserve Board of Governors in a 51–45 vote, with Senator John Fetterman the only Democrat in support; a separate procedural vote (51–45) advanced his nomination as Chair, with the final Chair confirmation vote expected Wednesday per Senate floor schedules and reporting from The Hill. Jerome Powell’s term as Chair ends Friday May 15 per the Federal Reserve calendar; he has previously stated he will remain on the Board after stepping down as Chair per CNBC. A confirmation today would land a new Fed Chair within 48 hours of yesterday’s hottest CPI print since 2023 and at the open of the Trump-Xi Beijing summit — a sequencing that compresses three of the largest macro-political variables (inflation re-acceleration, Fed leadership transition, and US-China-Iran negotiating posture) into a 72-hour window. Bond positioning ahead of the vote remained cautious with the 10-year near 4.46% per Reuters; CME FedWatch implies the new Chair inherits a market that has already largely priced policy hold or hike through 2026.
Trump-Xi Summit Opens Thursday with Iran on the Agenda — China Is Tehran’s Largest Oil Buyer and Trade Partner
Per CSIS, CNBC, and Council on Foreign Relations coverage, the Trump-Xi summit on May 14–15 in Beijing places Iran at the center of the agenda alongside trade, AI chip controls, rare earth exports, and Taiwan. Beijing is Iran’s largest trade partner and the top buyer of its oil, which gives China direct leverage on the Hormuz disruption that is now feeding US inflation prints; Treasury Secretary Bessent had earlier framed Iran as a primary topic per Atlantic Council coverage. President Trump sharply criticized the state of the ceasefire on Monday per ABC News and Fox coverage and dismissed Tehran’s counterproposal in strongly negative terms per The Washington Post and CNN coverage, and Reuters reported the President framing the objective of halting Iran’s nuclear program as outweighing the near-term economic cost to Americans. Pentagon officials have placed the cumulative US cost of the conflict at approximately $29 billion per Irish Times and ABC reporting. A US CEO delegation reportedly including Tesla’s Elon Musk, Apple’s Tim Cook, and Boeing’s Kelly Ortberg travels with the President per CFR coverage. For the IOWN portfolio, the summit is a four-way negotiation among the rare earths supply chain (semi sleeve), AI chip export licensing (NVDA, AMD, QCOM), oil flows (energy sleeve), and bilateral tariff posture. A constructive Iran read would tend to relieve the energy-inflation impulse; a stalemate would tend to extend it. Reuters also reported a magnitude 4.6 earthquake near Tehran with no casualties.
