Hot PPI Bypassed by a Tech-Led Tape — SPY +0.56% and Nasdaq +1.06% to Fresh Closing Records
The April PPI print landed materially above consensus and the tape rallied anyway. Per the BLS release and CNBC and CNN Business coverage, headline PPI rose 1.4% MoM — the largest single-month advance since March 2022 — and accelerated to 6.0% YoY from 4.0% in March, the steepest 12-month pace since December 2022 per the BLS release; core PPI excluding food and energy rose 1.0% MoM versus a 0.4% consensus. The upstream driver was the same energy passthrough that hit yesterday’s CPI: BLS reported final-demand energy prices climbed 7.8% with gasoline up 15.6% as pump prices crossed $4 per gallon during the survey window. Despite the upside surprise, SPY closed +0.56% at $742.31 with an intraday high of $743.91 and the Nasdaq (QQQ) added 1.06% to $714.71, both at fresh closing records per CNBC’s session coverage. The Dow (DIA) was modestly weaker at $497.14 (–0.15%), reflecting the rate-sensitive composition rather than a broad-market reversal. TLT (20Y Bond) declined 0.22% to $84.80, consistent with the front-end of the curve continuing to absorb a no-cut path: per The Washington Post and CNBC coverage of yesterday’s Senate floor action, Trump allies have publicly framed the rate-cut timetable as needing to wait through the energy-inflation impulse. UVXY ticked higher to $36.71 (+2.71%) in a session that was otherwise pro-risk — positioning kept a vol bid even as the cash tape advanced.

MRVL closed +8.18% at $177.95 — the day’s top IOWN mover — on two catalysts per Motley Fool and Yahoo coverage: AMD’s disclosure of a 65,516-share equity stake in Marvell (~$11.6 million per Quiver Quant) and Bank of America’s price-target raise to $200 from $125 citing AI-networking demand. The pattern is consistent with Tuesday’s reversal being absorbed by a buyer base that read the move as a valuation reset rather than a thesis break.

Section 02

Geopolitics

Warsh Confirmed Fed Chair 54–45 — Closest Vote in Modern Era — Powell’s Term Ends Tomorrow
The Fed transition is complete. Per CNBC, NPR, The Hill, and Bloomberg coverage of yesterday’s Senate floor action, Kevin Warsh was confirmed as the next Federal Reserve Chair in a 54–45 vote — characterized by CNBC as the closest Fed chair confirmation in the modern era — with Pennsylvania Democrat Senator John Fetterman the lone cross-aisle vote. The Washington Post reported Trump allies have publicly framed expectations that rate cuts may have to wait, an unusual signal from the administration given the President’s prior commentary on the Powell-era stance. Powell’s term as Chair ends Friday May 15 per the Federal Reserve calendar; he has stated he will remain on the Board per prior CNBC and NPR reporting. Warsh’s first FOMC meeting as Chair is scheduled for June 16–17 per Bloomberg. The transition lands within 48 hours of an April PPI print at 6.0% YoY and inside the open window of the Trump-Xi Beijing summit — a sequencing that places the new Chair in the position of receiving the May CPI and PPI prints in early June before his first policy meeting. The market reaction across the curve was modest: TLT –0.22% on the day, consistent with positioning that has been progressively converging on a no-cut 2026 path since the Iran war began.
Trump-Xi Summit Opens in Beijing — Xi Invokes “Thucydides Trap,” Names Taiwan as the Critical Issue
The summit is underway. Per CNBC, Al Jazeera, and CSIS coverage of this morning’s opening session, Xi opened by invoking the Thucydides Trap framing — the historical pattern under which a rising power and a ruling power slide into conflict — and stated that Taiwan was the most important issue for US-China relations, with state-media coverage warning that mishandling could push the bilateral relationship into dangerous territory. Trump described Xi’s leadership in positive terms in the opening remarks; Xi framed shared interests as outweighing differences. On Iran, Al Jazeera reported the analytical consensus that China’s help on Hormuz would likely require US concessions on Taiwan, and CNBC reported analyst views that Iran — while on the agenda — is not the central issue for either party at this summit. China remains the largest buyer of Iranian crude at over 80% of Tehran’s shipped exports per Al Jazeera reporting, which gives Beijing material leverage on the Hormuz disruption now passing through to US PPI and CPI. For the IOWN portfolio, the read-through is four-way: rare earths and chip export licensing (semi sleeve), tariff posture, Taiwan-related supply-chain risk concentrated in TSM, and the energy-inflation feedback loop. The Asian session traded constructive overnight; CNBC reported S&P futures +0.2% and Nasdaq futures +0.6% into Thursday’s open.
Hormuz Remains Effectively Closed — US Disabled Two Iranian Tankers May 8 as Pump Prices Cross $4.46/Gallon
The physical disruption that anchors the inflation impulse continues. Per Washington Post, NPR, and Al Jazeera coverage, US forces disabled two Iranian-flagged tankers (M/T SEA STAR III and M/T SEVDA) before they could enter an Iranian Gulf of Oman port on May 8, with Iran subsequently announcing the establishment of a Persian Gulf Strait Authority requiring full coordination and clearance for shipping through Hormuz. CNN cited an oil-market expert framing that gasoline could approach $5 per gallon if the strait remains closed; current pump prices sit near $4.46 per gallon per the same coverage, a level last seen nearly four years prior. Reuters reporting carried in the data drop noted the stress on the crude market and Asian banks raising provisions on credit risk as the conflict persists. The energy-sleeve tape on Wednesday was mixed despite the geopolitical anchor: XLE +0.10%, CVX +0.03% to $186.00, OKE +0.41% to $88.79, VLO –1.22% to $244.10, while FCX +1.71% to $67.16 continued to track the broader commodities complex. Oil ETFs eased into the summit: USO –1.57% to $142.04, BNO –1.83% to $55.85 — a marginal de-risking pattern consistent with positioning for a constructive read out of Beijing.
