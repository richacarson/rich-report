S&P Clears 7,500 for the First Time as the H200 Approval Drives a Concentrated AI Print — But the Substance Behind the Move Is Thinner Than the Tape Implies
Thursday delivered the first close above 7,500 for the S&P 500 per CNBC and Yahoo Finance coverage of the session, with SPY closing at $748.17 (+0.79%) per the data drop. The Dow reclaimed 50,000 (+0.74% to $500.80) and the Nasdaq added 0.71% to $719.79, all three benchmarks at record closes. The catalyst was Reuters’ report that the US Commerce Department had cleared sales of Nvidia’s H200 AI chips to roughly 10 Chinese firms including Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance, JD.com, Lenovo, and Foxconn, with per-customer caps of approximately 75,000 chips. NVDA closed at a record $235.74 (+4.39%), market cap moved to approximately $5.7 trillion per Yahoo coverage; TSM gained 4.48% to $417.72 on the read-through to AI-grade fab utilization. UVXY fell 3.49%, the bid for protection eased, and breadth modestly broadened as eight of eleven sectors closed positive — XLK led at +1.50% with XLE +0.76% and XLF +0.59% supporting. However, Friday’s setup is more mixed: Asian markets faded the rally per CNBC’s Asia wrap (Nikkei –1.1%, Hang Seng –0.89%), US futures softened (Dow –0.26%, S&P –0.37%, Nasdaq 100 –0.6% per Yahoo and CNBC pre-market coverage), and Benzinga and Reuters reported that Beijing has separately instructed Chinese firms to hold off on H200 purchases pending guidance — no actual shipments have been confirmed.
The Chip Complex Split Cleanly — NVDA, TSM, MRVL Higher on H200; QCOM Continues Its Reversal from Monday’s Record
The semiconductor sleeve traded the H200 thesis with sharp dispersion. The chips most directly levered to the Chinese AI build-out advanced: NVDA $235.74 (+4.39%), TSM $417.72 (+4.48%), MRVL $182.58 (+2.60%), AMD $449.70 (+0.94%), and LRCX $299.15 (+1.26%). The names with less direct exposure or with separate stories sold off: QCOM declined 6.14% to $200.08, extending the unwind from Monday’s $237.53 record — cumulative drawdown across the four sessions since the record now approximates 16%; NXPI –1.42% to $294.17, CRDO –2.55% to $184.54, and ADI –1.30% to $426.79. The pattern is consistent with positioning rotating into the names cleanest to the China-chip thaw narrative and away from the broader semi cohort that had previously absorbed flows on the earnings reset. CRDO’s give-back continues to look more like profit-taking than thesis impairment, with the company’s fiscal year-end print scheduled per its Finnhub release. NVDA prints next Wednesday, May 20 — the single largest catalyst of the next week for the entire sleeve.
Crypto and Retail-Trading Names Surge as Risk Sentiment Re-engages — HOOD +5.15%, COIN +5.06%, MARA +4.24%, IBIT +2.33%

HOOD ended at $80.70 (+5.15%) and COIN at $212.01 (+5.06%) — both within IOWN’s growth sleeve; MARA gained 4.24% to $13.29 and HUT added 0.97% to $109.37. CAT gained 1.99% on the Dow 50,000 milestone narrative per Yahoo. The dividend sleeve was mixed: ABT +1.28%, CL +1.10%, SYK +1.13%, NEE +0.88% on the constructive side; GPC –1.70%, DGX –1.52%, STLD –1.01% on the other.

Section 02

Geopolitics

The Beijing Summit Closes with Headline Commitments but Limited Substance — Boeing, Soybeans, Oil, and an Iran-No-Nukes Alignment
Per CNBC, Fox News, NBC News, and Al Jazeera coverage of the closing remarks at Zhongnanhai, President Trump and President Xi concluded their two-day Beijing meeting with claims of progress but few formally documented agreements. Trump told Fox News that China had agreed to order 200 Boeing aircraft; USTR Jamieson Greer said the US expects at least $10 billion in annual US agricultural purchases over three years on top of existing soybean commitments per Reuters. Trump separately said Xi pledged China would not supply Iran with military equipment and that the two leaders aligned on the proposition that Iran should not have nuclear weapons per Reuters. Xi reportedly expressed interest in increased Chinese purchases of US oil per CNBC. The White House readout per CNN coverage indicated discussion of trade, the Iran war, and the Strait of Hormuz; Xi separately warned Trump that mishandling Taiwan could risk escalating to direct conflict per France 24 and NBC coverage of the meeting. Foreign Policy characterized the summit as producing few clear wins; Al Jazeera framed expectations as having been low going into Friday. For the IOWN framework, the read is that the H200 export approval was the most concrete deliverable of the trip, but Beijing’s reported guidance to Chinese firms to hold off on chip purchases per Benzinga and Reuters tempers the near-term P&L translation.
Hormuz Re-escalates as the Summit Closed — Tanker Seized off UAE, Indian Cargo Ship Sunk off Oman, Brent on Track for ~+5% on the Week
Despite the Trump-Xi alignment that the Strait must remain open, the physical environment deteriorated. Per Washington Times, Euronews, NBC News, and NPR reporting on Thursday and Friday, a tanker was seized approximately 38 nautical miles north of Fujairah, UAE, by unauthorized personnel and was tracked heading toward Iranian waters; the UK Maritime Trade Operations is investigating. Separately, the Indian-flagged cargo ship Haji Ali sank off the coast of Oman after an attack ignited a fire onboard; all 12 crew were rescued per Oman coast guard reporting. Per Reuters via OilPrice.com, Brent crude was trading above $106 on Friday and was extending its weekly gain as diplomatic efforts continued to stall and the Strait remained effectively under selective Iranian passage controls. Within the IOWN energy sleeve, the Thursday session showed selective response: CVX +0.34%, OKE +2.52% to $91.03, CNX +2.80% to $36.40, and VLO +0.27%; XLE added 0.76% on the day. The structural read is that the Hormuz disruption premium remains embedded in crude even as the diplomatic framework for resolution remains nominal rather than operational.
