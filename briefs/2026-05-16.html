Section 02

Geopolitics

Israel and the US Reportedly in Intense Preparations for Renewed Strikes on Iran as Early as Next Week — The Beijing Summit Did Not Deliver an Off-Ramp
The dominant weekend development is The New York Times reporting, carried by The Times of Israel’s May 16 liveblog and Haaretz’s contemporaneous coverage, that Israel and the United States are in the most intense preparations since the April ceasefire for renewed strikes on Iran, with planners reportedly targeting readiness as early as next week. Per the same coverage citing US officials, options under consideration include placing ground commandos to extract nuclear material from buried facilities or to seize Kharg Island, Iran’s primary oil export hub in the Persian Gulf. The reporting follows what Reuters and Al Jazeera characterized as a wall in Trump’s Iran diplomacy: Tehran’s response to the latest US ceasefire proposal, conveyed via Pakistan mediators on May 10 per Al Jazeera, was rejected by Trump as unacceptable, and Trump separately told Reuters his patience with Iran has limits. The Beijing summit produced limited substance against high expectations — China agreed to order 200 Boeing aircraft and US officials cited an expected ~$10B/yr US agricultural purchase commitment per Reuters and CNBC, but Al Jazeera noted the US and Chinese summit readouts overlap only in narrow areas. The Israel–Lebanon ceasefire was extended 45 days per Reuters as Washington concluded parallel talks. If the renewed-strikes reporting is taken at face value, the operational picture would suggest the diplomatic window is now narrow.
Brent Closed Friday Above $109 and Added 8.1% on the Week — The IEA Now Warns the Oil Market May Remain Materially Undersupplied Through October Even If the Conflict Resolves Next Month
The energy backdrop is the most direct portfolio expression of the geopolitical risk. Per Trading Economics and Reuters coverage on Friday, Brent crude rose to approximately $109.24/bbl (+3.33% on the day) and added 8.1% on the week as the Strait of Hormuz remained effectively closed; per the House of Commons Library briefing and the Strait of Hormuz monitoring data, open transits have fallen to near zero per day since May 6 versus roughly 138 vessels daily pre-crisis. Per the IEA monthly oil market report and Trading Economics coverage, crude and fuel flows through the Strait fell by approximately 4 million bpd in March and April; the agency warned the global oil market could remain materially undersupplied through October even if the conflict resolves next month. Iraq’s Hormuz oil exports fell to roughly 10 million barrels in April from approximately 93 million monthly pre-war per Reuters reporting. The IOWN energy sleeve captured the move on Friday with CVX +2.39% to $191.10, VLO +2.44% to $250.74, and OKE +1.42% to $92.32; XLE was the lone winning sector at +2.36%. Brent (BNO) and WTI (USO) closed Friday at $57.69 (+2.63%) and $148.23 (+3.66%) respectively in the data drop.
