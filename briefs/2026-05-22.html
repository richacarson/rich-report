Section 02

Geopolitics

The De-escalation Track the Prior Brief Tracked Met a Hardening From Tehran — Iran’s Supreme Leader Directed the Country’s Stockpile of Near-Weapons-Grade Enriched Uranium to Remain Inside Iran, Rejecting the Overseas Transfer That Washington Has Made a Central Demand of the Peace Talks
The dominant geopolitical development is a hardening rather than a collapse. Per Reuters reporting, Iran’s Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei issued a directive Thursday that the country’s stockpile of uranium enriched to roughly 60% purity should not be sent abroad — directly challenging one of Washington’s central demands in the Pakistan-mediated peace talks, where US officials have insisted the near-weapons-grade material be removed, transferred, or neutralized as part of any agreement. Per the same reporting, Iranian officials view shipping the material out as leaving the country more exposed to future US or Israeli strikes. President Trump responded that the United States intends to see the highly enriched uranium removed regardless, characterizing it as material Washington neither needs nor wants but will not allow Iran to retain, and reiterated that Iran is absorbing heavy economic cost from the US naval posture — which he put at roughly $500 million a day. Separately, per reporting, the Gulf authority overseeing the strait stated that transit through Hormuz now requires coordination with and authorization from that authority, consistent with the pattern of Iran consolidating control of the waterway the prior brief described. The de-escalation has not reversed, but the gap between the two sides on the nuclear file has visibly widened.
The Market Read the Hardening as Negotiating Posture Rather Than Re-escalation — Crude Gave Back Its Intraday Spike, the IOWN Energy Sleeve Was the Weakest for a Second Session, and the War-Risk Premium Continued to Unwind Even as the Underlying Geopolitical Risk Was Not Retired
