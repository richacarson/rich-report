President Trump Stated Saturday That a Framework With Iran — Including a Reopened Strait of Hormuz — Had Been Largely Negotiated and Would Be Announced Shortly — Per Reporting, the Contemplated Terms Include a Sixty-Day Ceasefire Window, Mine-Clearing in the Strait, a Lifted US Port Blockade, and Parallel Two-Month Negotiations on the Nuclear File, Though Iran’s Foreign Ministry Has Disputed That the Nuclear Track Is Inside the Current Discussion
The dominant weekend development materially shifts the negotiating posture the prior brief described. Per CNBC, NPR, PBS, and CBS coverage, President Trump posted Saturday that a deal with Iran had been largely negotiated following calls with leaders from Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, Qatar, Pakistan, Turkey, Egypt, Jordan, Bahrain, and separately with Israel, with final terms expected to be announced shortly. Per Axios reporting Sunday, the framework under discussion contemplates a sixty-day ceasefire window during which the Strait of Hormuz would be reopened with no tolls, Iran would clear the mines it deployed in the waterway, the US would lift its port blockade, and sanctions waivers would allow Iran to sell oil; parallel two-month negotiations on the nuclear file would proceed alongside. Per the same coverage, the deal has not been finalized and could still fall apart. The two structural gaps the prior brief flagged remain at least partially open. First, per Times of Israel reporting Saturday, Iranian state media contradicted the Hormuz reopening framing, saying the strait would remain under Iran’s management. Second, per Iran’s Foreign Ministry spokesperson cited in CBS coverage, nuclear issues are not part of current negotiations from Tehran’s perspective — with the focus instead on ending the war and lifting sanctions. The market read on Sunday evening would depend on whether a formal announcement materializes.
