Section 02

Geopolitics

14-Point MOU Under Negotiation — The Closest to a Deal Since February 28
The US and Iran are negotiating a 14-point memorandum of understanding to end the war, according to Axios and CNN. Secretary of State Rubio described the deal as “still a work in progress,” while Iran’s Foreign Ministry stated that “conclusions have been reached on many topics” but an agreement is not imminent, per Reuters. The framework would include Iran’s commitment to address its highly enriched uranium stockpile (though Iranian and US sources offer conflicting accounts of what has been agreed), reopen the Strait of Hormuz with demining, and establish a 60-day window for final deal points. Nuclear negotiations have been deferred to a later phase. Iranian state media reported that differences persist over one or two clauses. Trump stated negotiations are “proceeding nicely.” Xi praised Pakistan’s mediation efforts and Iran’s engagement, while Netanyahu admitted difficulty influencing Trump’s decisions on Iran, per Reuters. For markets, the MOU represents the most concrete path to resolution since the conflict began—oil continues to decline on peace expectations, with Brent falling 0.97% on Friday.
The Supply Chain Is Already Adapting — India Pivots to Latin America and Africa
India has turned to Latin American and African oil sources after the Hormuz disruption, per Reuters, while Russian crude volumes to India hit record levels during the conflict. The global energy supply chain has been permanently rerouted by three months of Hormuz disruption, and even a peace deal would not immediately reverse the structural changes. For IOWN’s energy sleeve, this means CVX, VLO, OKE, and CTRA benefit from both the near-term disruption premium and the longer-term supply chain reconfiguration that the war has catalyzed.
