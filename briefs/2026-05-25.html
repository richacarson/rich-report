Geopolitics

The Forty-Eight Hours Since Saturday’s Announcement Have Introduced Visible Friction in Both Capitals — Iran’s Foreign Ministry on Monday Stated Per Times of Israel Coverage That an Agreement Is Not Imminent and Accused Washington of Shifting Positions, While President Trump on Sunday Stated Per CNBC and Fortune Reporting That the US Would Not Rush a Deal and Spot Crude Repriced Lower by Roughly Five Percent
The Saturday framework has not collapsed, but the path to signature has narrowed in two specific ways since the prior brief. First, on the Iranian side, per Times of Israel reporting Monday, Iran’s Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei conveyed that while Tehran and Washington have reached conclusions on a large portion of the issues under discussion, no party is in a position to assert that a signature is imminent — with Baghaei separately accusing Washington of shifting its positions. Per the same coverage, both sides are now framing the contemplated outcome as a memorandum of understanding lasting sixty days and extendable by mutual consent rather than as a finalized agreement. Second, on the US side, per CNBC and Fortune reporting Sunday, President Trump characterized the negotiations as proceeding in a constructive and unhurried posture while making clear the US would not rush a deal. The combined signal — Tehran tempering expectations Monday, Washington signaling patience Sunday — reads as a deliberate decompression of the near-term-announcement framing Saturday’s Truth Social statement had carried.
The Israeli Track Surfaced Publicly Sunday for the First Time When Prime Minister Netanyahu Per Axios and CNN Coverage Conveyed That Iran Would Never Be Permitted to Acquire a Nuclear Weapon — The Reported Trump-Netanyahu Saturday Phone Call Centered on Israeli Concerns Over Hormuz Control and the Nuclear File, With Trump Reportedly Reassuring That He Would Stand Firm on Dismantling Iran’s Nuclear Program and Removing All Enriched Uranium
The Israeli posture has not been quiet through the weekend. Per Axios and CNN reporting Sunday, Prime Minister Netanyahu publicly responded to the emerging framework for the first time, conveying that Iran would not be permitted to acquire a nuclear weapon and that any final agreement must eliminate the nuclear danger. Per the same coverage, Netanyahu convened coalition leaders and security chiefs to discuss the terms; one Israeli official characterized the emerging deal as giving Iran too much control over the Strait of Hormuz, and another official conveyed Netanyahu’s message to Trump that Israel would still act against perceived regional threats — including in Lebanon, where strikes have continued. Per CNN’s account of the Saturday Trump-Netanyahu call, Trump reportedly reassured Netanyahu that he would stand firm in negotiations on the dismantling of Iran’s nuclear program and the removal of all enriched uranium. The two structural gaps the prior brief tracked — Tehran’s pushback on Hormuz management and Iran’s position that the nuclear file is not part of current negotiations — now run alongside an Israeli track that has surfaced publicly. None of these gaps individually retire the framework; together they explain why the market has begun to price the deal partially rather than fully.
