Section 02

Geopolitics

The MOU Survives the Holiday Weekend — Trump Calls It “Proceeding Nicely” While Tehran Disputes the Hormuz Control Language
The 14-point memorandum of understanding the prior brief flagged remained on the table through the long weekend without finalization, per CNN and Al Jazeera coverage. President Trump stated on Monday that talks were “proceeding nicely.” The framework as currently described contemplates a roughly sixty-day ceasefire extension during which the Strait of Hormuz would be reopened, Iran would be able to freely sell oil under sanctions relief, and parallel negotiations on the nuclear file would proceed. The unresolved seams remain material: per Reuters and Al Jazeera, Iranian state media continues to dispute that Hormuz would shift outside Tehran’s management; the timing of sanctions relief and the scope of the nuclear track remain points of friction; and frozen Iranian assets have surfaced as an additional sticking point. The market read on Tuesday morning is that the framework is intact enough to keep oil compressing — Brent traded near $96 per barrel overnight per Korea Times and CNBC coverage as Iran framework progress drove a multi-day drift lower — but not yet finalized enough to fully unwind the war-risk premium.
The IOWN Energy Sleeve Sits at the Hinge — Downstream and Integrated Names Diversify the Sleeve Through the Transition
A confirmed Hormuz reopening within the sixty-day window would, per Wood Mackenzie commentary previously referenced, point spot Brent toward $80 by year-end as the war-risk premium unwinds; Goldman Sachs has cited a late-2026 view closer to $90 on more persistent disruption. The sensitivity is already visible in Friday’s tape: CTRA closed at $32.56 (–8.62%), CNX at $35.32 (–0.65%), with USO and BNO both posting weekly losses on progress signals. For the integrated and downstream names — VLO at $246.98 (↑2.44% Friday) and CVX at $191.45 (↑0.23%) — the profile diversifies the sleeve’s exposure relative to the upstream-and-gas concentration. The structural case for the energy sleeve does not reset on a procedural framework alone; the “physical world matters” thesis was built around constrained capacity and supply discipline rather than around an escalation path. Defense exposure (LMT at $533.26, ↑2.00% Friday; GD at $342.91, ↑1.24%) retains an orthogonal-hedge role through the transition window — a sixty-day ceasefire is not a permanent retirement of the underlying risk.
