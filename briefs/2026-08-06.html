Section 02

Geopolitics — Hormuz Slips, Lebanon Flares

The Iran-Oman Framework Advances Without a Signature
Per CNBC and Washington Times coverage of the Wednesday sequence, Iran and Oman characterized the Strait of Hormuz talks as in their final stages. The proposed framework calls for commercial vessels to enter the Persian Gulf via an Iranian-controlled route and exit via an Oman-controlled route, with service fees charged for security and environmental preservation. President Trump reiterated per CNBC that a deal could materialize as early as Wednesday or Thursday, while an Iranian official denied direct US-Iran negotiations were underway per the same coverage — the same denial-confirmation choreography that has framed the last two weeks of price action. Crude priced through the sequence with WTI (USO) closing ↓0.78% at $114.88 and Brent (BNO) ↑0.20% at $45.41; the equity read was in the FCI energy sleeve, with CVX ↓2.10% at $186.41, CNX ↓3.11% at $34.54, and OKE ↓0.47% at $87.24.
Israel Strikes South Lebanon
Per Reuters headlines captured in the data drop, Israel struck south Lebanon after Israeli soldiers were killed, with parallel diplomatic talks continuing in Rome. The escalation reopens a channel the market had largely priced as dormant through July and adds a second live theater to the existing Iran-Hormuz posture. The FCI defense sleeve traded softer despite the headline, with LMT ↓1.99% at $577.60 and GD ↓0.44% at $384.08 — a composition consistent with a market that continues to treat the primary catalyst as the Hormuz reopening rather than a broadening regional conflict. Should the Lebanon channel escalate materially, that asymmetry would likely reverse.
The Sinopec Signal
Per Reuters exclusive reporting captured in the data drop, Sinopec stepped up Russian oil imports to offset Middle East supply cuts, according to traders and cargo trackers. The flow reroute is meaningful as a slow-moving signal on how the physical crude architecture is adapting to the current disruption sequence — incremental Russian barrels moving east ease the marginal-buyer pressure that would otherwise anchor the front-month bid. Combined with the OPEC+ September 188,000 bpd add-back and the pending Hormuz reopening scenario, the physical-crude setup continues to loosen at a pace that is not fully reflected in the front-month tape.
