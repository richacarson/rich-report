A Week That Ended on the Labor Print
The week closed with an outsized macro surprise. Per BLS coverage, July nonfarm payrolls printed −23,000 against Continuum Economics consensus near +80,000, with May revised down to +63,000 (from +129,000) and June revised down to +20,000 (from +57,000). The unemployment rate ticked down slightly to 4.1%, and per NBC and CNBC coverage, the composition featured a −53,000 government drag with additional softness in retail, leisure and hospitality. Equities took the print as a dovish reset, closing the week higher across the major averages, and precious metals rallied alongside as a market pricing a softer Fed reaction function retained its geopolitical hedge tied to unresolved Hormuz.

Geopolitics — Mecca Pact, Hormuz Still Open-Ended

Saudi Arabia, Turkey, Pakistan Sign the Mecca Joint Defence Agreement
Per CBS News, Washington Post, and Al Jazeera coverage of the Friday signing, Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, Turkish President Erdogan, and Pakistani Prime Minister Sharif signed a trilateral collective-defence pact in Mecca. Per the coverage, an armed attack against any of the three states is to be treated as an attack against all three, and the arrangement builds on the September 2025 bilateral Saudi–Pakistan defence agreement while extending comparable commitments to Turkey. Per Al Jazeera coverage, Turkish media reporting flagged provisions likely to enable joint military exercises, technology transfer, and intelligence sharing, with a Turkish government official characterizing the pact as purely defensive in nature. If taken at face value, the signing consolidates a Sunni-majority security architecture running from Ankara through Riyadh to Islamabad at precisely the moment the Iran–Hormuz negotiations remain unresolved and the Yemen theater re-escalates per Reuters coverage of Houthi strikes on Marib. For the FCI defense sleeve (LMT, GD), the directional read is supportive of long-cycle Gulf-region order flow, though specific procurement composition remains undisclosed.
Hormuz Framework Still in the Corridor Construction
Per Reuters coverage captured in the data drop, a US official flagged expectations of an Iran–Oman deal on the Strait of Hormuz in the near term, though the operational rollout remains undisclosed. The corridor construction previously reported by the Washington Post — commercial vessels entering via an Iranian-controlled route and exiting via an Oman-controlled route in exchange for service fees — leaves meaningful physical friction intact rather than restoring the pre-disruption flow. Crude gave back a portion of Thursday’s reversal bid on Friday but remains elevated versus the pre-Hormuz baseline. The Mecca pact signing and the still-open corridor operational detail set up a headline-sensitive Sunday–Monday tape, with Iran’s public response to the trilateral pact the primary weekend variable to watch.
Russia Sanctions Package Clears the Senate
Per Reuters, Washington Post, and NPR coverage captured in the data drop, the Senate passed the Russia sanctions package, which was renamed the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026 in honor of the late Senator; the House is scheduled to take up the measure next. The advance is a slow-moving policy vector that has developed alongside the Iran–Hormuz sequence rather than in reaction to it, and the composition of the eventual signed legislation — particularly any secondary-sanctions provisions on buyers of Russian crude — would introduce a distinct physical-supply variable relevant to the current oil setup. Progression through the House is a live weekend watch item as parliamentary-procedure detail typically leaks during recess periods.
