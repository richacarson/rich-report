Section 02

Geopolitics — Jazan Strike, Iran’s Six Demands, and a Public Trump–Netanyahu Split

Houthi Drone Hits Aramco’s Jazan Refinery — Fire Extinguished, No Injuries
Per Reuters, Al Jazeera, NPR, and Al Arabiya coverage of the Sunday statement, Houthi forces claimed a drone strike on Saudi Aramco’s Jazan refinery in southwestern Saudi Arabia, framing the strike as retaliation for Saudi drone incursions into Saada and Hajjah governorates. Per Aramco disclosures cited by Reuters, the fire was extinguished by industrial security firefighting teams with no injuries reported at the 400,000 barrel-per-day facility. Simultaneous Houthi attacks per NBC coverage struck the Yemeni Red Sea port at Mokha, killing seven and wounding thirty per Yemeni military statements captured in the data drop headlines. The Jazan strike arrives two days after the Saudi–Turkey–Pakistan Mecca defence pact and against the backdrop of unresolved Hormuz negotiations, materially extending the Yemen-theater risk perimeter into Saudi refining capacity. For the FCI energy sleeve (CVX, CNX, OKE), the strike introduces a discrete supply-side variable that is distinct from the Hormuz transit friction that has framed the crude tape since late February.
Iran Publishes a Six-Point Demand List on Hormuz Reopening
Per Reuters coverage captured in the data drop and CNN coverage of the Saturday statement from Iran’s Supreme National Security Council, Tehran published a formal six-point list of preconditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz: end threats against Iran’s Supreme Leader, halt US military action against Iran and its regional allies, withdraw US naval and air forces enforcing the blockade, compensate for war damages, lift US sanctions, and release frozen Iranian assets. The publication converts what had been a corridor-construction negotiation with Oman into a broader precondition framework. Per Reuters coverage of the Sunday oil tape, the framing pushed crude modestly higher on the sense that a rapid reopening is now unlikely. For FCI positioning, the six-point list re-anchors the Hormuz analytical framework: prior briefs had characterized the Iran–Oman corridor as leaving physical friction intact; the six-point list suggests the friction is now the intended state, not a rollout artifact.
Netanyahu Publicly Rejects Trump’s 15-Point Gaza Plan
Per CNBC, NBC News, Washington Post, and Reuters coverage captured in the data drop, Israeli Prime Minister Netanyahu on Sunday publicly rejected Trump’s Board of Peace 15-point plan for Gaza, framing his objection around the disarmament sequence: Israel does not withdraw before Hamas surrenders heavy and light weaponry, and Netanyahu emphasized that only genuine disarmament, not partial or symbolic surrender of arms, would meet the precondition per NBC coverage. Per CNN analysis, Netanyahu is caught between far-right coalition ministers angered by prior concessions and his primary military backer, Trump, ahead of an October 27 election. If taken at face value, the public rejection introduces visible daylight between the White House and the Netanyahu government on the terms of a Gaza framework. For the FCI defense sleeve (LMT, GD), a stalled Gaza framework combined with the Pentagon’s Wednesday memo demanding accelerated munitions production supports the long-cycle order-flow directional read, though specific procurement composition remains undisclosed.
